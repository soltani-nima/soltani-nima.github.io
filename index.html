01About

About

Learning Systems That Must Act Before the Evidence Is In

I study Computer Engineering at Shahid Beheshti University and work as a research assistant at the Institute for Research in Fundamental Sciences (IPM) and at Shahid Beheshti University. My work asks how learning systems should decide when the signals they depend on are heterogeneous, structured, and incomplete.

At IPM, this means reinforcement-learning agents for trading and portfolio decisions that combine technical, fundamental, and textual inputs and model market structure with graph and hypergraph networks; physics-informed Kolmogorov–Arnold networks for partial differential equations; and deep operator networks for power-system dynamics. The work has led to manuscripts under review at Elsevier and IEEE journals and an arXiv preprint. At Shahid Beheshti University, I study Ethereum protocols and smart-contract security, and analyse MEG recordings, from preprocessing and source localization to neural decoding, to study visual perception and cognitive processing.

Separately, my current research focus is the intersection of vision and action, especially deception: how deceptive intent is expressed in an action and read by an observer. Alongside the research, I teach as an assistant at Sharif University of Technology, Amirkabir University of Technology, and Shahid Beheshti University, in courses from first-year programming to graduate level.

03Research

Research

Research Areas and Open Questions

Vision, action, and deception

Perception and action are usually studied apart, yet every reach is planned from what the eyes report, and every movement changes what is seen next. Deception strains that loop. An actor can shape a movement to hide its goal; an observer has to infer the goal from the movement alone. The questions I am working toward:

  1. How do visual perception and action planning shape each other while a movement is prepared and carried out?
  2. When an action is meant to deceive, how is the hidden intent expressed in the movement, and how is it read by an observer?
  3. What does an observer rely on to see through a deceptive action, and how early in the movement does the intent become readable?

Visual perceptionAction planningDeceptionIntent inference

Trading agents that read the whole market

Prices are only part of what moves a market. Company fundamentals, the language of financial news, and the way assets move together all carry information. This line builds deep reinforcement-learning agents that take those signals in together, with graph and hypergraph neural networks modelling the relations among assets inside the agent rather than as features added afterwards. It underlies papers [1] and [2]. The directions:

  1. How should technical, fundamental, and textual signals be fused so that an agent’s decisions improve, not only its inputs?
  2. Which sequence backbones, such as xLSTM and transformers, give a trading policy the memory it needs in nonstationary markets, and how can diffusion policies be trained conservatively enough to stay stable?
  3. How can graph and hypergraph networks capture co-movement and group structure among assets in a form the agent can use when it allocates a portfolio?

Networks bound by their equations

A network fitted only to data can match its samples while breaking the physics that produced them. Physics-informed learning puts the governing equations into training. In PG-KINN [4], a Petrov–Galerkin weak formulation is paired with Kolmogorov–Arnold networks, whose learnable univariate functions replace fixed activations, to solve forward and inverse PDE problems in one framework. The same idea, applied to operators, carries the power-systems work [3]. The directions:

  1. When does a weak, Petrov–Galerkin form of the residual train more reliably than the pointwise residual of standard physics-informed networks?
  2. What do the learnable univariate functions of Kolmogorov–Arnold networks offer for representing PDE solutions, compared with fixed activations?
  3. How can one framework solve the forward problem and recover unknown coefficients in the inverse problem without separate machinery for each?

PINNsKolmogorov–Arnold networksPetrov–GalerkinWeak formulationInverse problems

Fast, physics-consistent power-system dynamics

After a disturbance, whether a generator stays in step with the grid is decided in the first seconds of its swing. Checking that across many operating conditions means solving the same equations again and again. Paper [3] learns those solutions as an operator: a physics-informed DeepONet for the classical swing dynamics of a single-machine infinite-bus system, trained to respect the swing equation and its initial state. The directions:

  1. Can one operator network replace repeated numerical solves of the swing equation across initial states and mechanical set-points while staying consistent with the physics?
  2. How should initial conditions and the swing-equation residual enter training so that predicted rotor-angle trajectories can be trusted for transient-stability screening?
  3. What would it take to extend such surrogates from the classical single-machine model toward richer machine models and multi-machine systems?

Power systemsTransient stabilitySwing equationDeepONetSurrogate modelling

From raw MEG to decodable signal

Magnetoencephalography records the brain’s magnetic fields with millisecond precision, but what reaches the sensors is a mixture of sources, noise, and artefacts. At Shahid Beheshti University, this work builds the path from raw recordings to interpretable signal for studying visual perception and cognitive processing. The directions:

  1. Which preprocessing choices, such as filtering, artefact rejection, and epoching, keep the signal of interest while removing what is not neural?
  2. How can activity measured at the sensors be localized to cortical sources reliably enough to support conclusions about visual and cognitive processing?
  3. What do spectral and time–frequency analyses, together with decoding models, reveal about when and where perceptual information is represented?

MEGPreprocessingSource localizationNeural decodingTime–frequency analysis

Systems that must hold against adversaries

A blockchain is a protocol that has to keep working while some participants try to break it. At Shahid Beheshti University, I study Ethereum from that angle: how consensus and protocol rules shape what transactions can do, how on-chain activity can be modelled, and where smart contracts and DeFi infrastructure are exposed. The directions:

  1. How do consensus mechanisms and protocol design choices in Ethereum constrain what honest and adversarial participants can do?
  2. How can on-chain transactions be modelled so that unusual or exploitative patterns stand out?
  3. Which classes of vulnerability recur in Solidity smart contracts and DeFi protocols, and how can analysis and careful design catch them before deployment?

  • Linear Algebra

    SVD, PCA, and Numerical Methods in Python

    Practical sessions connecting the theory to code: decompositions computed in NumPy, PCA on real data, and why numerical stability matters.

  • Advanced Programming

    Java OOP, C++ STL, and Game Development in Unity

    Classes, interfaces, and polymorphism in Java; STL containers, iterators, and algorithms in C++; and a small 2D Unity game in C# organised with object-oriented patterns.

    BFS, DFS, and shortest paths, then turning problems into recurrences and solving them with memoisation or tabulation.

  • Data Mining

    Hands-on sessions on preprocessing, feature engineering, clustering, and classification, with attention to evaluation and to the pitfalls of leakage.

  • Machine Learning Systems Engineering

    Reproducible Machine-Learning Pipelines

    Experiment tracking, configuration management, and versioned data and models, through to the basics of packaging and deploying a trained model.

  • Principles of Design and Analysis of Experiments

    Randomisation, blocking, and factorial designs, with simulation used to reason about effect sizes, power, and analysis of variance.

  • Blockchain Technology

    Ethereum Smart Contracts and Security
