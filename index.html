01Overview

A research practice between models, markets, and measurement

I am an undergraduate in Computer Engineering at Shahid Beheshti University and a research assistant at the Institute for Research in Fundamental Sciences (IPM), where I work with Dr. Dara Rahmati on reinforcement learning for financial markets and on physics-informed networks.

The common thread is learning from signals that do not share a single form: price series beside fundamentals, differential equations beside data, graphs beside sequences. I care about architectures that respect that structure, and about evaluations careful enough to tell when they do not.

At Shahid Beheshti I study Ethereum protocols and smart-contract security with Dr. Maedeh Mosharraf, and work with Dr. Narjes Soltani on MEG: how visual perception and action planning interact, and how deceptive intent shows up in action and in neural signals. Teaching runs alongside all of it, at Shahid Beheshti, Sharif, and Amirkabir University of Technology.

Current focus · Computational neuroscience

Vision, action, and deception

Seeing and acting are usually studied apart, yet every reach is planned from what the eyes report, and every movement changes what is seen next. Deception puts that loop under strain: an action can be shaped to hide its own goal, and an observer has to read intent from motion alone. With Dr. Narjes Soltani at Shahid Beheshti University, I am working toward questions like these:

  1. How do visual perception and action planning shape each other while a movement is prepared and carried out?
  2. When an action is meant to deceive, how is the hidden intent encoded in the actor, and how is it perceived by an observer?
  3. Can that intent be decoded from MEG — from localized sources and their time–frequency dynamics — and how early?

Decision agents that read more than price

Reinforcement-learning traders that combine price dynamics with fundamentals and financial text, built on transformer and xLSTM backbones, with conservative on-policy diffusion to keep portfolio behaviour stable.

Graph and hypergraph neural networks that treat co-movement, influence, and regime structure as first-class objects in time-series and market modelling, rather than as features added after the fact.

Networks that know the equations

Physics-informed Kolmogorov–Arnold architectures, including PG-KINN, for forward and inverse PDE problems — where a Petrov–Galerkin formulation and learnable univariate functions are designed to work together.

Reading cortical dynamics

MEG pipelines from preprocessing to source localization and decoding, using spectral and time–frequency analysis to study activity linked to visual perception and cognitive processing — the groundwork for the vision–action questions above.
